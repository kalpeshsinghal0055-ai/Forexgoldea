MT4 vs MT5 for a gold EA: the honest platform comparison

Every new gold EA user hits the same fork in the road: MetaTrader 4 or MetaTrader 5? The internet is full of tribal answers. The honest one is shorter and more useful — here it is, difference by difference.

In short For most gold EA users, MT5 is the better platform today — a far superior strategy tester (real tick data, multi-threaded), faster execution, and it's where broker development is heading. MT4 is still perfectly fine if your broker or EA is MT4-only. ForexGoldEA runs on both, so the practical rule is: backtest on MT5, run on whichever platform your broker account gives you.

The five differences that actually matter for EA users

MT4 and MT5 look similar on the surface — charts, Navigator panel, drag an EA on, done. The differences that matter live underneath:

Backtesting: MT5 wins, and it isn't close

If you test strategies before trusting them — and on gold you absolutely should — MT5's tester is the single biggest reason to prefer it. It supports "every tick based on real ticks" natively, runs optimisations across all your CPU cores, and can test multi-symbol portfolios. Getting MT4 to a comparable 99% modelling quality means importing tick data with third-party tools.

For gold specifically this matters more than usual, because XAU/USD's volatility punishes sloppy tick modelling. Our full walkthrough: how to backtest a gold EA properly.

Execution and speed: MT5 by a nose

MT5's execution engine is newer — more order types, better partial-fill handling, and generally faster processing. For a slower rule-based EA the difference is modest; for anything fast-moving on gold it adds up. Either way, your broker's execution quality and your VPS latency matter more than the platform's engine.

The hedging-mode trap (the one real MT5 gotcha)

Check this before running any EA on MT5: MT5 accounts come in hedging mode (separate buy/sell positions, like MT4) or netting mode (positions merge into one). An EA that manages individual positions can misbehave on a netting account. Most brokers default retail accounts to hedging mode — but verify in your account settings.

Broker support: the practical decider

Here's the truth that settles most cases: you'll use whichever platform your broker account gives you. Most brokers now offer both, with newer account types increasingly MT5-first. MetaQuotes stopped issuing new MT4 broker licences years ago — MT4 isn't dying tomorrow, but the direction of travel is one-way. If you're opening a fresh account today with no constraints, choose MT5 and skip a future migration.

Reader questions

Should I run my gold EA on MT4 or MT5?

MT5 for most users — better tester, faster engine, future-proof. MT4 is still fine if your broker or EA requires it.

Will one EA file work on both platforms?

Not the same file — MQL4 and MQL5 are different. Providers like ForexGoldEA supply separate builds for each platform.

Is backtesting better on MT5?

Significantly: native real-tick modelling, multi-threaded optimisation, multi-symbol tests. MT4 needs third-party tick data to compete.

Does MT5 support hedging?

Yes, in hedging mode. Netting-mode accounts merge positions — check which your broker account uses.

Will MT4 stop working?

No announced shutdown — existing servers keep running — but no new MT4 broker licences are issued, so the ecosystem is slowly migrating.

Which platform does ForexGoldEA run on?

Both MT4 and MT5, with identical strategy and risk rules. Use whichever your broker provides.

Where this leaves you

Don't overthink it. MT5 is the better toolkit — especially its backtester — and the platform the industry is moving to; MT4 is the familiar workhorse that still does the job. Since ForexGoldEA runs identically on both, let your broker account decide where you run, and let MT5 be where you test. The robot's rules, your risk settings and your broker's spreads will decide your results — the platform logo won't.
